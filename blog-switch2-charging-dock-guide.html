Switch 2 Charging Dock: The Complete Buyer's Guide (2026)

A Switch 2 charging dock is a dedicated station that charges the console, Joy-Con controllers, and Pro controllers in one place, using USB-C power

Charging Dock · November 12, 2026 · 5 min read · Category: Charging Dock

Quick answer

A Switch 2 charging dock is a dedicated station that charges the console, Joy-Con controllers, and Pro controllers in one place, using USB-C power instead of the single port on the console itself. The right model depends on three decisions: which devices you need to charge at once, whether you want a desktop or wall-mounted setup, and whether you need extra storage or RGB lighting. This guide covers every type sold in the current 15-model Switch 2 range and how to choose between them.

What is a Switch 2 charging dock?

A Switch 2 charging dock (also called a charging base or charging station) is a powered accessory that holds and charges one or more Switch 2 devices at the same time. The console itself has a limited number of charging ports, so a dock expands how many controllers can be charged together and keeps them organized on a desk or wall.

In the current JINGOX range, a Switch 2 charging dock can charge up to four Joy-Con controllers on a single unit, while multi-functional bases also accept Pro controllers and the console. The exact number of simultaneous devices is the first specification to check on each model.

Which devices can a Switch 2 dock charge?

A Switch 2 setup includes the console, two Joy-Con controllers (one left, one right), and optionally a Pro controller. Docks are built around different combinations:

  • Joy-Con only: the 4-in-1 dock (HBS-2824) charges four Joy-Con at once; the Joy-Con charging base (HBS-2875) is a compact option for a pair.
  • Pro controllers: the dual Pro controller dock (HBS-2908) charges two Pro controllers, and the multi-functional base (HBS-2862) handles both Joy-Con and Pro.
  • Console and controllers together: multi-functional docks such as HBS-2804 and HBS-2806 combine console charging with controller slots and storage.

How to choose a Switch 2 charging dock

Work through these five specifications in order.

  1. Number of devices charged at once. Count the Joy-Con and Pro controllers you use; a household with four players needs a 4-in-1 or multi-functional dock, while a single pair only needs a compact base.
  2. Desktop vs wall-mounted. Desktop docks sit on a shelf and usually add storage; wall-mounted docks free up desk space and display the console. Compare both on the wall-mounted vs desktop page.
  3. Powered USB-C input. Every dock requires its own USB-C power supply; confirm the unit is powered rather than a passive stand.
  4. Lighting and style. RGB models (HBS-2811, HBS-2849) suit a gaming setup; plain docks are lower cost for resale bundles.
  5. Cross-generation compatibility. Choose HBS-2879 or HBS-2881 if the dock must also serve Switch 1.

Installation and safety

Desktop docks simply plug into a USB-C power source and accept the controllers in their slots. Wall-mounted docks use a bracket fixed to the wall, with the console or controllers held securely in place.

For wall mounting, check that the bracket supports the weight of the console and that the charging cable can reach a power outlet. The safety and installation checklist is covered on whether wall-mounted docks are safe for Switch 2.

Frequently asked questions

Can one dock charge Joy-Con and Pro controllers together? Yes. Multi-functional bases such as HBS-2862 accept both Joy-Con and Pro controllers; confirm the exact device count on the model page.

Do RGB docks charge the same devices as standard docks? RGB lighting is a style feature and does not change which devices fit. Compare the two on the RGB vs non-RGB page.

What is the difference between a charging dock and a charging grip? A dock stays on a desk or wall and charges multiple devices; a grip (HBS-2827) holds two Joy-Con as a handheld controller while charging. See the dock vs grip comparison.

Will a Switch 2 dock work with Switch 1? Only the cross-generation models HBS-2879 and HBS-2881 are designed for both; Switch 2-specific docks are not interchangeable.
