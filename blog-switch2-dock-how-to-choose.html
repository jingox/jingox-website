How to choose a charging dock for Switch 2

Choose a Switch 2 charging dock by first counting the devices you need to charge at once, then deciding between desktop and wall mounting, and finally

Charging Dock · November 12, 2026 · 3 min read · Category: Charging Dock

Quick answer

Choose a Switch 2 charging dock by first counting the devices you need to charge at once, then deciding between desktop and wall mounting, and finally checking power input, lighting, and whether you need Switch 1 compatibility. The current range has 15 Switch 2 docks, from a compact Joy-Con base to a multi-functional station that charges Joy-Con, Pro controllers, and the console together. Matching the dock to your exact device count is the single most important step.

Step 1: Count the devices you charge at once

List every device in the setup: the console, two Joy-Con controllers, and any Pro controllers. A four-player household needs a dock that accepts four Joy-Con, such as the 4-in-1 HBS-2824 or a multi-functional base like HBS-2806. A single pair only needs a compact base such as HBS-2875.

Step 2: Choose desktop or wall-mounted

Desktop docks sit on a shelf and usually add storage slots for games and controllers. Wall-mounted docks such as HBS-2807 and HBS-2855 free up desk space and display the console. The full trade-off is on the wall-mounted vs desktop page.

Step 3: Confirm a powered USB-C input

A charging dock must connect to its own USB-C power source. A passive holder without a powered input does not charge anything, so confirm the unit is a powered dock rather than a plain stand.

Step 4: Decide on lighting and style

RGB docks HBS-2811 and HBS-2849 add lighting for a gaming setup, while standard docks keep cost down for resale bundles. Lighting does not change which devices fit.

Step 5: Check cross-generation support

If the dock must also serve Switch 1, choose HBS-2879 or HBS-2881. Switch 2-specific docks are not interchangeable with the older console.
